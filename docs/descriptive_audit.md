# Descriptive and figure audit

All 44 Table 1 proportions, bias measures, and treatment contrasts reproduce the
manuscript's reported precision. All seven response counts and seven published
respondent counts also reproduce. Machine-readable comparisons are in
`tabs/table_1_benchmarks.csv` and `tabs/table_1_count_benchmarks.csv`, generated by
`scripts/07_validate.R`. These checks validate point estimates and counting rules;
they do not establish exact equivalence of the bootstrap draws or standard errors.

## Denominators and respondent counts

The published proportions divide the number of congenial, correct, or uncongenial
answers by the number of classifiable answers. Treatment effects are differences
in those response-level proportions. This reproduces the saturated-logit contrasts
in the original bootstrap helper programs without fitting an unnecessary logit.
Bias is congenial minus uncongenial, so its treatment contrast is the difference
between two response-proportion contrasts.

The Table 1 respondent count uses raw, nonmissing answers in the wide file, including
negative refusal codes. Consequently, Study 1 reports 242 control and 229 monetary
respondents, although only 237 and 227 contribute a classifiable response. The pooled
reported counts are 455 and 430, versus 450 and 428 contributing respondents. Study 2
counts coincide under both definitions: 213 control, 246 appeal, 201 monetary.
This seven-person distinction must be shown when describing the analytical sample;
it does not change the reproduced response proportions.

## Bootstrap sampling frames

The initial R implementation jointly resampled respondents across arms after removing
missing outcomes. That is a valid explicit analytical-sample bootstrap, but not a
verified translation of every legacy command. Legacy arm-specific `summarize` calls
have an `if` qualifier; missing outcomes within that qualifier do not themselves
establish an estimation sample. The wrapper programs for contrasts and bias invoke
`logit` or `proportion`, which may leave `e(sample)` behind. Their declaration as
`rclass` alone does not establish that Stata ignores that sample.

Stata's manual states that `bootstrap` drops observations outside an available
`e(sample)` and explicit qualifiers unless `nodrop` is requested. When an estimation
sample is unavailable, other missingness exclusions cannot be inferred automatically.
The exact legacy wrapper behavior therefore requires a Stata run, or inspection of
the matching Stata-version prefix implementation. Until then, record the chosen
sampling frame and distinguish analytical-sample and full-frame sensitivity results.
See the [Stata 13 bootstrap manual](https://www.stata.com/manuals13/rbootstrap.pdf)
and [Stata's missing-values FAQ](https://www.stata.com/support/faqs/statistics/resampling-and-missing-values/index.html).

A matching seed does not make R and Stata random streams identical. Compare estimates
at published precision, but evaluate standard-error differences with Monte Carlo
uncertainty and sampling-frame evidence. The benchmark table deliberately labels
standard-error validation as pending instead of passing an arbitrary tolerance.

## Figure 2 smoothing

The raw category indicators and cumulative probabilities in the R plotting code
agree with the legacy construction; its `acc` variable excludes Study 1 by design.
Both Bush-reference conditions enter the knowledge plots, matching the original
commands. Missing classifiable answers remain missing instead of becoming zero.

R's `lowess(f=..., iter=0, delta=0)` does not reproduce Stata's neighborhood rule.
`R/smoothing.R` now implements the index-centered window and weighted local linear
fit documented in the [Stata 13 LOWESS formulas](https://www.stata.com/manuals13/rlowess.pdf).
Stata truncates neighborhoods at the data boundaries; R's documented algorithm uses
nearest neighbors. The [R LOWESS documentation](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/lowess.html)
explains its different rule.

For raw bias panel C, switching from R's nearest-neighbor implementation to the
Stata-formula implementation changes smoothed probabilities by as much as 0.01937
in control and 0.02402 under incentives. At the low-knowledge endpoint, incentive
bias changes from 0.09937 to 0.07536. These are translation differences, not changes
to the input data, item choice, or knowledge scoring.

Six smoothing assertions test linear reproduction, the boundary-window formula,
missingness, and tied knowledge values. Input order is retained within ties; exact
agreement with a particular historical Stata sort remains unverified. Do not claim
pixel-level figure replication from these formula tests alone.

Figure A1 uses respondent-level rather than answer-level data. Weighting each
respondent by 100 divided by party sample size correctly expresses percentages.
The modern integer-width bins differ from Stata's unspecified default histogram
bins; treat this as a presentation choice, not proof that the historical histogram
coordinates have been reproduced.
The [Stata 13 manual](https://www.stata.com/manuals13/g-2graphtwowayhistogram.pdf)
specifies a default bin count based on `min(sqrt(N), 10 * log10(N))` and a
starting point at the observed minimum. The manuscript code leaves those defaults
implicit. Without a runtime check of integer conversion and bin-edge handling,
the archive keeps its explicit one-point bins and discloses the difference.

## Headline claims

- H1's descriptive component reproduces: pooled bias declines from 11.4569% to
  6.07499%, a 5.38194-point reduction. The incentives reduce reported partisan
  response bias in these observed-answer samples. Statistical significance still
  depends on the validated uncertainty calculation.
- In Study 2, monetary and appeal effects have similar point estimates. A test
  that does not reject equality is not an equivalence test and cannot establish
  that both interventions are equally effective to any specified tolerance.
- H2's claimed attenuation must be distinguished from the reported interaction
  test (one-sided p=.23). A nonsignificant interaction does not establish that
  partisan cues reduce the incentive effect, even if one subgroup estimate is
  significant and another is not.
- H3's knowledge moderation requires validation of the multinomial fit and its
  predictions. Reproducing the published knowledge score and smoothing formula
  does not validate that measurement as the only defensible knowledge construct.
  Alternative scoring and item inclusion are examined in the separate knowledge audit.
- The final July 29 recode explicitly derives post-randomization dropout and
  reproduces the appendix’s eight Study 2 dropouts. The earlier July 15
  13-versus-eight discrepancy is superseded, not an error in the canonical release.

No claim about underlying beliefs, motive, or knowledge follows solely from matching
treatment effects on reported answers. Such interpretations require the paper's
additional assumptions about response behavior and available information.

An independent cluster delta-method calculation provides a numerical check on the
bootstrap: all 44 standard errors are within three approximate Monte Carlo standard
errors of that calculation (`tabs/bootstrap_validation.csv`). This supports the
scale of the R uncertainty estimates, but does not settle the exact nested-wrapper
sampling frame used by the original Stata version. Bootstrap-frame counts are named
explicitly and should not be confused with Table 1's analytical respondent counts.

The canonical July 29 `PSK_Functions.do` and bootstrap/LOWESS commands are unchanged
from the earlier extracted copy. The final Figure 1 hardcoded intervals do change;
comparisons must use that final file and its `PSK_JAGS.R` source.

## Published significance wording

The typeset paper and final DOCX agree on the audited tables and headline passages.
The pooled congenial-response claim p < .001 conflicts with Table 1's reported
SE precision: even its smallest rounding-compatible SE gives p = .001046 using
the exact replicated effect and the stated one-sided normal test. Current bootstrap
and independent cluster delta calculations give .001918 and .001466. The pooled
reduction remains significant at .01. The accuracy-appeal prose and table symbols
also disagree around .05; rounding permits either side of that threshold, so its
original significance classification remains unresolved. See
`publication_verification.md` and `tabs/prose_significance.csv`.
